    How to read this

    Full definitions at thestrat.ai/docs.

    The cake is the crypto market. Each tier is one layer of crypto, from base money at the bottom to Wall Street at the top. Each slice of a tier is one market. Its color shows who controls the price bar: green = buyers, red = sellers, dark = nobody yet. The arcs on the right show rotation: out of the layers where sellers are in control, into the layers where buyers are. The plate under the cake is the supply of stablecoins, the dollars inside crypto: green when new dollars arrive, red when dollars leave.

    Two figures are not price. The stablecoin supply (the plate) is tracked money: one value a day from DefiLlama, made into bars the same way as price. The market value shares come from CoinGecko at the time of the last update: "by value" gives every dollar one vote, where the counts give every market one vote. The shares are for coins only, not for the Wall Street tier.

    Limits. The arcs show which side is pushing price, not money that physically moves. Every trade has a buyer and a seller. Stablecoins have no slice, because their price stays at 1.00; they are the plate. The price history goes back 10 years at most, so a coin that started in 2024 or 2025 has only 2 or 3 yearly bars. Coins trade every day; the Wall Street tier trades on weekdays only, so on a weekend it shows Friday's bar. Bars still trading are live and can change until they close.
